# Research behind the rules

Use this when rules conflict and you need to judge which one matters more for this reader.

## Core principle

Readers have limited time and working memory. Writing that lowers the effort to understand gets read and acted on.
(Rogers & Lasky-Fink, *Writing for Busy Readers*, 2023; Sweller, cognitive load theory)

## Words

- Needlessly complex vocabulary makes writers seem less intelligent and less credible. (Oppenheimer, 2006)
- Experts overestimate what readers know, which is the "curse of knowledge". This is why coined labels and undefined jargon fail. (Camerer, Loewenstein & Weber, 1989; Hinds, 1999)
- Concrete words and examples are understood and remembered better than abstractions. (Paivio, dual coding theory; Sadoski et al.)
- Verbs carry action more clearly than nominalizations. (Williams, *Style*)

## Sentences

- Familiar information first and new information last matches how readers integrate text. (Clark & Haviland, 1977; Gopen & Swan, 1990)
- Long gaps between subject and verb, and nested clauses, overload working memory. (Gibson, dependency locality theory, 1998)
- Readers expect the main character as subject and their action as verb. (Williams, *Style*)

## Paragraphs and flow

- Explicit connectives help most when the reader is new to the topic. (McNamara et al., 1996; Kintsch, construction-integration model)
- Interesting but extraneous material hurts comprehension. (Mayer, coherence principle)

## Length and asks

- Shorter messages with fewer ideas get read and acted on more often. (Rogers & Lasky-Fink, field experiments)
- Telling readers early why the content matters to them raises engagement. (Rogers & Lasky-Fink)

## Navigation

- Readers scan for the point and read selectively. (Nielsen Norman Group eye-tracking studies)
- Informative headings and other signals improve recall of a text's structure and gist. (Lorch & Lorch, signaling research)

## Formatting

- Formatting helps scanning up to a point; past that, everything looks equally important. (Rogers & Lasky-Fink)
- Bullets strip the logical links between ideas, so reasoning belongs in prose.
